# VELQ | Expanded finance industry map

Draft native Whop lesson, 3 November 2026. High-level orientation; validate against each actual team and vacancy. Office labels describe typical positioning, not status or a universal hierarchy.

## The three office labels

Front office commonly refers to client-facing or investment/revenue activities. Middle office commonly refers to independent risk and control or supporting functions near the business. Back office commonly refers to processing, settlement, records and administration. The labels vary, overlap and can obscure skilled work. Technology, actuarial, corporate finance and consulting do not map neatly across organisations.

## Investment banking

TYPICALLY FRONT OFFICE / ADVISORY & FINANCING

Advise companies and financial sponsors on transactions and financing. Analyst work can involve research, financial modelling, valuation, presentations and managing transaction details.

Overlap: Corporate finance and private markets share company analysis and valuation. Banking advises clients on transactions; investors decide whether to commit capital.

Potential development: Research corporate advisory, capital markets or sector coverage. Later moves depend on experience, performance and available roles.

Start here: explain financial statements, enterprise versus equity value and one valuation approach.

## Asset management

INVESTMENT TEAMS / TYPICALLY FRONT OFFICE

Research investments and build or support portfolios for clients. Work varies by asset class and may combine company analysis, macro research, portfolio construction and risk monitoring.

Overlap: Equity research develops investment views; risk teams challenge exposures. Portfolio management adds allocation, sizing and client mandate constraints.

Potential development: Explore research analyst, investment specialist, multi-asset or portfolio support routes. Portfolio responsibility normally develops with experience.

Start here: compare two investments and discuss return drivers, risks and portfolio fit.

## Wealth management

CLIENT & INVESTMENT ROLES / OFTEN FRONT OFFICE

Help individuals and families organise investments and financial objectives. Roles can focus on relationship management, investment advice, portfolio management or financial planning.

Overlap: Asset management shares investment analysis. Wealth adds personal circumstances, suitability, client communication and longer-term relationships.

Potential development: Explore assistant relationship manager, investment analyst and planning pathways. Advice responsibilities depend on training and regulatory permissions.

Start here: explain risk tolerance, diversification and how you would clarify a client’s needs.

## Risk management

OFTEN MIDDLE OFFICE / INDEPENDENT OVERSIGHT

Identify, measure and challenge exposures such as market, credit, liquidity and operational risk. Work can range from data analysis and modelling to limits, governance and reporting.

Overlap: Quant, treasury, compliance and investment teams all use risk information. Independent risk has a distinct challenge and oversight role.

Potential development: Explore market, credit, model or enterprise risk. Career paths depend on technical depth, product knowledge and the kind of risk you specialise in.

Start here: describe a loss scenario, its drivers, controls and the limits of a risk measure.

## Sales & trading

TYPICALLY FRONT OFFICE / MARKETS

Support clients’ market activity through sales, trading, structuring or execution. Tasks depend on product and desk, and combine market knowledge, communication, risk and pricing.

Overlap: Research informs views; quant supports models and pricing; risk provides oversight. Sales and trading are distinct roles with different daily emphasis.

Potential development: Explore desk-specific sales, trading, structuring and electronic markets. Product knowledge and client needs shape progression.

Start here: explain a recent market move and how it could affect a client or trading position.

## Quantitative finance

FRONT OR MIDDLE OFFICE / TEAM DEPENDENT

Use mathematics, statistics and programming to analyse data, model products or develop investment and risk methods. Quant research, development and model validation are different jobs.

Overlap: Trading, systematic investing, risk and technology share coding and models. The objective and who uses the model determine the role.

Potential development: Explore quant research, pricing, model validation or quantitative development. Entry requirements can be substantially mathematical.

Start here: implement a small model, explain its assumptions and test its behaviour.

## Equity & credit research

INVESTMENT RESEARCH / OFTEN FRONT OFFICE

Analyse companies, industries or issuers and form a reasoned view. Equity research focuses on ownership and valuation; credit research focuses on repayment, downside and debt terms.

Overlap: Banking also analyses companies, while asset management uses research to make portfolio decisions. Research depth, audience and incentives differ.

Potential development: Explore sell-side or buy-side research, sector coverage and credit analysis. Moving to portfolio roles requires further investment responsibility.

Start here: build a short investment or credit thesis with valuation, risks and what would change your view.

## Private equity & venture capital

TYPICALLY FRONT OFFICE / PRIVATE MARKETS

Assess investments in private companies, support due diligence and monitor portfolio businesses. Buyout, growth and venture strategies have very different investment criteria.

Overlap: Banking shares transaction and valuation skills. Venture can emphasise markets, founders and growth; buyouts place greater weight on cash flow, leverage and operations.

Potential development: Explore investment and portfolio-support roles. Direct undergraduate opportunities exist but vary; prior transaction or operating experience can matter.

Start here: assess a business model, investment rationale, main risks and due-diligence questions.

## Corporate banking

TYPICALLY FRONT OFFICE / CORPORATE CLIENTS

Work with companies on lending, cash management and related banking services. Roles connect credit analysis, sector understanding, client needs and relationship management.

Overlap: Investment banking focuses more on transactions and capital markets; corporate banking often centres on ongoing banking relationships and credit.

Potential development: Explore coverage, credit analysis and product teams. Experience can lead to relationship management or more specialised financing work.

Start here: assess a company’s ability to service debt and explain a relevant banking need.

## Treasury

CORPORATE FINANCE OR BANK TREASURY / VARIES

Manage cash, liquidity, funding and financial exposures. Corporate treasury supports a business; bank treasury manages the institution’s balance sheet and funding needs.

Overlap: Risk measures exposures; finance reports performance; markets teams execute instruments. Treasury connects those functions to liquidity and funding decisions.

Potential development: Explore cash management, liquidity, asset-liability management or funding. Responsibilities depend on the organisation.

Start here: explain cash versus profit and how interest-rate or currency risk affects a business.

## FP&A & corporate finance

INTERNAL FINANCE / OFFICE LABELS OFTEN UNHELPFUL

Support business decisions through budgeting, forecasts, performance analysis and financial planning. Some corporate finance teams also handle investment appraisal and transactions.

Overlap: Accounting provides actual results; strategy sets direction; treasury manages cash and funding. FP&A turns financial information into operating decisions.

Potential development: Explore financial analyst, business partnering and planning roles. Broader finance leadership normally requires substantial experience.

Start here: explain a variance, forecast a driver and turn the result into a business recommendation.

## Actuarial

INSURANCE, PENSIONS & CONSULTING / VARIES

Use statistics and financial modelling to assess uncertain long-term costs and risks. Work may cover insurance pricing, reserving, capital, pensions or investment advice.

Overlap: Risk and quant share modelling; consulting adds client communication. Actuarial work has its own professional qualification and practice context.

Potential development: Explore insurance, pensions, investment consulting or risk routes. Qualification requirements and study support vary by employer.

Start here: explain a probability model, its assumptions and how you would communicate uncertainty.

## Compliance & financial crime

OFTEN MIDDLE OFFICE / CONTROL FUNCTIONS

Help firms understand obligations, manage conduct and financial-crime risks, and monitor controls. Compliance, legal and internal audit are related but separate functions.

Overlap: Risk, operations and business teams implement controls. Compliance interprets and challenges conduct and regulatory processes; audit independently evaluates them.

Potential development: Explore advisory compliance, monitoring, anti-money laundering or sanctions. Specialisation follows the business and regulatory area.

Start here: work through a control failure, evidence requirements and a sensible escalation path.

## Operations & trade support

OFTEN BACK OR MIDDLE OFFICE / VARIES

Make financial activity work reliably: settlements, reconciliations, asset servicing, collateral and exception management. Some roles sit close to desks; others support firm-wide processing.

Overlap: Trading initiates activity; technology builds systems; operations ensures records and processes reconcile and issues are resolved.

Potential development: Explore product operations, trade support, control and process improvement. Moves across functions depend on demonstrated skills and opportunities.

Start here: explain a trade lifecycle, investigate a mismatch and propose a control.

## Finance technology & data

CROSS-FUNCTIONAL / TEAM DEPENDENT

Build and maintain software, data pipelines and analytical tools used by financial firms. Engineering, data science, analytics and production support have different responsibilities.

Overlap: Quant uses models; operations needs reliable workflows; every business needs sound data. Technology focuses on robust systems and delivery.

Potential development: Explore software engineering, data engineering, analytics and platform roles. Product knowledge can complement technical expertise.

Start here: build a small data workflow with clear documentation, checks and reproducible output.

## Financial services consulting

CLIENT ADVISORY / OFFICE LABELS VARY

Help financial institutions solve strategy, risk, technology or operational problems. Work often combines research, analysis, stakeholder conversations and recommendations.

Overlap: Specialist risk, finance and technology knowledge can be used in advisory work. Consultants support a client’s decision rather than owning every ongoing function.

Potential development: Explore specialist consulting teams and project delivery. Later paths depend on the capabilities and industry knowledge you build.

Start here: structure an ambiguous problem, prioritise analysis and present a defensible recommendation.

## Make the differences concrete

IB advises on a transaction; AM selects and manages investments within a mandate. Research develops evidence and views; PM adds allocation, sizing and portfolio constraints. Wealth focuses on individual client circumstances and relationships. Risk independently measures and challenges exposures. Trading handles market activity; operations makes the trade lifecycle reliable. Corporate banking supports ongoing banking and credit relationships, while treasury manages funding and liquidity.

## Your task

Choose three areas. Find one current official vacancy for each. Compare responsibilities, output, team, eligibility and one skill gap. Write one reason to explore and one question to resolve. Movement between roles is possible but never automatic.

## Editorial scope

Role fit is a hypothesis. This lesson does not promise an exit opportunity, professional qualification or job. Investment and relationship roles within the same division can differ significantly. Hedge funds are a strategy/employer category within investment management, not one uniform graduate job; sales, research, portfolio, risk, quant and operations roles can exist within them.
