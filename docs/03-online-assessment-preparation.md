# VELQ | Online assessments: prepare with a method

Draft native lesson · 3 November 2026

You do not need to become perfect at every kind of test. You need to understand the format you face, find your weak point and practise it deliberately.

## 1. Read the invitation before you practise

Record the employer, role, provider, exact test name, deadline and time zone. Check duration, calculator or paper rules, whether items can be revisited and what support is permitted. If you need an adjustment, contact the employer or provider early. Never infer rules from another firm’s test.

Use official familiarisation materials first. Aon provides candidate guidance and practice tasks:
https://www.aon.com/en/capabilities/talent-and-rewards/prepare-for-your-online-assessment

This is a provider-specific resource, not the format of every recruitment test. Preparation plans below are VELQ’s suggested study methods.

## 2. Numerical reasoning: build accuracy, then pace

Refresh percentages and percentage changes; distinguish percentage points from percentage growth. Practise ratios, proportions, averages, rates, currency conversions, tables and graphs. Write units beside intermediate results and estimate the expected size before calculating.

Example A — growth: revenue rises from £80m to £92m. Increase = £12m; percentage growth = 12 ÷ 80 × 100 = 15%.

Example B — percentage points: a margin rises from 20% to 25%. That is +5 percentage points and a 25% relative increase in the margin, because 5 ÷ 20 = 25%.

Example C — weighted average: 30 units cost £4 each and 20 cost £7 each. Total cost = £260; average = £260 ÷ 50 = £5.20 per unit. Averaging £4 and £7 without weights gives the wrong answer.

Example D — units: a chart records revenue in £000. A figure of 250 means £250,000, not £250. Check headings before interpreting a result.

Your error log should identify: question type; your approach; interpretation error, calculation error or timing issue; correct reasoning; and a follow-up drill. Do not simply write “careless”.

Suggested 35-minute session: 5 minutes reviewing one concept; 12 minutes of representative practice following the stated rules; 13 minutes reviewing and reworking errors; 5 minutes choosing the next drill. Adapt the timing to your needs. This is practice scheduling, not a claim about test duration.

## 3. Verbal reasoning: evidence before intuition

For true / false / cannot say formats, use only the supplied passage and definitions in the test. True means the statement follows from the passage; false means it is contradicted; cannot say means the evidence does not settle it. Some providers use different labels or rules, so read the instructions.

Original practice: “All analysts in Team A completed the induction. Some analysts in Team B completed it.”
- “Every analyst in Team A completed the induction.” True.
- “No analysts in Team B completed the induction.” False.
- “Team A has more analysts than Team B.” Cannot say.

Review whether you imported outside knowledge, confused “some” with “all”, or reversed a logical implication.

## 4. Inductive and deductive reasoning

For patterns, examine counts, position, rotation, shading and alternation separately. Test your proposed rule against every visible step. For rule-based logic, list constraints, distinguish necessary from possible and check counterexamples. Avoid choosing a plausible answer before testing it.

## 5. Situational judgement and work simulations

Identify the objective, stakeholders, authority and constraints. Consider ownership, communication, evidence and proportionate escalation. Read whether you must rank, rate or choose responses; there is no universal answer letter or one rigid rule that works in every situation. For personality questionnaires, respond honestly rather than trying to engineer a profile.

## 6. Protect your own performance

Check equipment, connection and permitted materials. Leave enough time for instructions and logistics. Use authorised adjustments where needed. Follow the actual rules on AI and outside assistance; do not use this guide or another person to answer a live assessment if prohibited. If something fails, record the issue and contact the stated support route.

## 7. Close the loop

After practice, record one repeatable mistake and one next drill. After the recruitment stage, record the format, your own preparation and any feedback you are permitted to retain. Do not copy confidential assessment items.

Your finished output: one preparation plan, one error log and one next action. No preparation resource can guarantee a pass.
