# VELQ | Technical preparation: choose the right depth

Draft native lesson · 3 November 2026

Technical preparation works best when it follows the work of the team. Start with the actual vacancy and your current level; do not spend every evening trying to cover all of finance.

## Use the explain → apply → challenge loop

1. Explain the idea in plain language without notes.
2. Apply it to a calculation, business example or small piece of code.
3. Challenge it: assumptions, limitations, sensitivities and what could change your conclusion.
4. Record what you cannot yet explain and practise that gap next.

## Investment banking and company analysis

Build a sound understanding of the three financial statements, cash versus profit, enterprise versus equity value, valuation approaches and basic transaction mechanics. Practise connecting an assumption to a valuation result. Read an actual company’s disclosures rather than memorising isolated definitions.

Original drill: a business has £100m equity value, £30m debt and £10m cash. Under the simplified definition enterprise value = equity + debt − cash, enterprise value is £120m. Explain why enterprise value and equity value answer different questions; actual valuation adjustments can be more complex.

## Asset management and research

Practise explaining an investment thesis, valuation, catalysts, downside and what would change your mind. For portfolio questions, discuss diversification, objectives, constraints, allocation and position sizing at an appropriate level. A view without risks is incomplete.

Original drill: two companies have similar growth expectations but different prices and balance sheets. What evidence would you need before calling one the better investment? Separate a good company from a good investment at its current price.

## Wealth management

Practise explaining risk, diversification and investment choices clearly. Begin with client goals, horizon, liquidity needs and circumstances; do not jump straight to a product recommendation. Learn the role’s boundaries and escalate questions beyond your training or permissions.

## Risk

Understand the risk types relevant to the role and practise discussing scenarios, exposures, controls, limitations and model assumptions. For quantitative measures, be able to explain what a metric does and does not describe.

Original drill: a portfolio’s historical risk measure falls. What could still make the portfolio vulnerable? Consider changed exposures, liquidity, concentration, data windows and unusual market conditions.

## Sales and trading

Learn the desk’s products and client purpose. Practise a concise market explanation: what moved, possible drivers, what is verified, what is your interpretation and how a client or position could be affected. Pricing and derivatives depth depends on desk and programme.

## Quantitative and technology roles

Follow the role’s mathematical and programming requirements. Practise probability, statistics, linear algebra, data analysis or algorithms where relevant. Write code you can explain, test edge cases and discuss data leakage, assumptions and limitations. Requirements vary greatly between research, development and validation.

## Corporate banking, treasury and FP&A

For corporate banking, practise cash-flow and debt-service analysis. For treasury, explore liquidity, funding and interest-rate or currency exposure. For FP&A, practise forecasts, business drivers, variances and turning analysis into an operating recommendation.

## One focused 45-minute session

Choose one concept (5 minutes). Explain it aloud (10). Apply it to a small example (15). Test the result and answer two follow-ups (10). Record the next gap (5).

Keep evidence of your learning: a short model, calculation, concept card or recorded explanation. Do not claim expertise based on memorising a definition. These are illustrative preparation routes, not a universal interview syllabus.
